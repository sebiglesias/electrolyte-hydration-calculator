⚠️ Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia (EAH) & Renal Advisory

Hyponatremia Hazard (Water Intoxication): Overconsumption of plain hypotonic water without adequate sodium during events lasting longer than 3–4 hours can dangerously dilute blood serum sodium (< 135 mmol/L), resulting in Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia (EAH), cerebral edema, seizures, and death.

Cardiovascular & Renal Medications: Individuals taking ACE inhibitors, Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs), or potassium-sparing diuretics must exercise extreme caution with high-potassium electrolyte formulations due to the risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Clinical Dehydration: This calculator is designed for exercise fluid management and is not a substitute for clinical oral rehydration therapy (ORT) for medical cholera or pathological diarrhea.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my personal sweat rate?
Weigh yourself nude immediately prior to training. After your session, towel dry, weigh yourself nude again, and record fluid drank: Sweat Rate (oz/hr) = [(Pre-Weight - Post-Weight in lbs) × 16 + Fluid Drank in oz] ÷ Workout Hours.
Why is plain water dangerous during prolonged endurance marathons?
Sweat contains high concentrations of sodium (800–1,600 mg/L). Drinking gallons of sodium-free water during prolonged events dilutes blood sodium levels below 135 mmol/L, causing life-threatening Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia (EAH).
How much fluid should I drink after working out?
ACSM guidelines recommend drinking 125% to 150% of the net fluid lost over the 2 to 4 hours following exercise, alongside sodium, to fully restore intracellular and extracellular fluid volumes.
